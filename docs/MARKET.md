# CARTA 7 — the market, framed as play

*Rank stated first: this document does not drive the ship. Carta is a hobby;
Lotmark is the business (`COMMERCE.md` was the third turn's commerce brief
and goes with it). This framing exists for one purpose — direction: knowing
the landscape tells us what to lean toward and what to turn away from, the
way a sailor reads other boats without racing them. If a line here ever
argues against a joy in `PIVOT.md`, the joy wins.*

## 1. The landscape, honestly read

Coffee software keeps choosing one of four corners, and the corners explain
what Carta is by what it isn't:

- **The lab bench** — brewing loggers (Beanconqueror and its kin): open,
  earnest, data-maximal. Every variable capturable, dashboards everywhere.
  The audience is the tinkerer; the failure mode is homework. Classic Carta
  drifted toward this corner with better typography. Carta 7 keeps the
  dial-in loop and refuses the dashboard.
- **The check-in** — Untappd/Vivino-shaped taste-social: log it, rate it,
  feed it, badge it. Huge, sticky, and precisely the gamified sociality the
  design brief's audience is allergic to. Nothing to take except the
  cautionary tale.
- **The directory** — café guides and specialty maps (European Coffee Trip
  and the like) plus generic Google/Yelp mass. Coverage without taste: they
  can say what exists, never what clears *your* bar. This corner is also
  where Lotmark's verified atlas will eventually outclass everyone — which
  is Lotmark's fight, not Carta's.
- **The diary done right** — Letterboxd. The one product in any taste
  domain that proves the whole shape Carta 7 wants: a personal log that is
  a pleasure to keep, lists as artifacts, a year-review people *post*, and
  a business that never needed a feed algorithm to matter. Letterboxd is
  the spiritual kin; coffee simply doesn't have one.

And the real incumbent for the founding use case is none of these: it is
**the AI chat itself**. Everyone's travel-coffee concierge is now a model
with no memory of their palate. That is the gap Carta sits in — not
another app corner, but the *corpus layer* the concierge era is missing.

## 2. The one durable position

Models will keep getting better at "best cafés in Lisbon." They will not
get better at knowing **you** — what earns your 9s, which houses anchor
your taste, what you've already had. That knowledge only exists if someone
keeps it, and the keeper should be an instrument the person owns outright:
offline, exportable, beautiful, loyal to no platform. **As concierge AI
commoditizes recommendations, the private taste corpus appreciates.**
Carta is that corpus with a great coat on. This position gets *stronger*
with every model release — a rare property, and the reason the hunt is
built brief-first: the brief works with whatever AI the founder already
trusts, today's or next year's.

## 3. Lean toward

- **Letterboxd's lesson**: the diary is the product; the year-review is the
  marketing; lists (chapters, passports, briefs) are the social object.
- **Artifacts as the growth loop.** Cards spread hand to hand like a good
  bag recommendation, and each carries its own data (`carta.card/v1`) so a
  recipient can start a Carta from a gift. If Carta ever grows, it grows
  the way sourdough starter does.
- **The traveler.** Nobody serves the person who plans a trip around
  coffee. The route ask alone is a product no corner offers.
- **The high bar as identity.** Carta 7 is unembarrassed about being for
  the top of the market — "the absolute best light roast in the world" is
  a sentence the app should be able to say with a straight face.
- **The Lotmark adjacency.** Every menu captured, every roaster graphed is
  upstream signal for the business next door. Carta can be Lotmark's
  consumer showcase and taste-side demo without carrying its machinery.

## 4. Turn away from

- **Feeds, follows, likes, streaks, badges.** The moment Carta needs
  engagement, it has died as a hobby and failed as a brand.
- **Coverage contests.** Carta never competes on knowing every café; it
  competes on knowing *you*. Coverage is the directory corner's game and
  Lotmark's future moat.
- **Aggregate ratings.** A café's Carta score across users is Untappd with
  serifs. Scores stay personal; the shared object is the brief, which is
  signed by a palate, not averaged from a crowd.
- **B2B of any kind** — café dashboards, roaster tools, claimed pages.
  All of it is Lotmark. Carta holds one side of the counter only.
- **Ads, affiliates, sponsored placement.** Never. The old seal's first
  clause survives every turn: we do not sell coffee — anyone's.
- **Venture framing.** No TAM, no growth targets, no "platform." This
  document is the closest Carta gets to a market strategy, on purpose.

## 5. If it accidentally resonates (the pressure valve)

Hobbies sometimes catch. If strangers turn up, the paths that don't corrupt
the product, in order of comfort:

1. **Nothing.** A free, beautiful, single-file app with export is allowed
   to just be that. The costs are static hosting; the founder can afford a
   hobby.
2. **Patronage** — a tip jar, an edition of printed year-cards. Aligned by
   construction.
3. **The concierge tier** — the stage-two scout run on Carta's key for
   people who won't bring their own (`ROADMAP.md` horizon). The one
   revenue shape that sells the product's own virtue: precision about
   *you*. It is opt-in compute, never a data business — the corpus never
   leaves the device except inside the keeper's own ask.

The lines never crossed, at any scale: selling coffee, selling placement,
selling readings, requiring accounts, or letting anything become the
business that makes Carta stop being play. That last one is what Lotmark
is for.

## 6. The only metric

Does the founder still open it happily? Everything else — the old
north-star return-rate stays a pleasant curiosity to compute on the Year
card — is commentary.
